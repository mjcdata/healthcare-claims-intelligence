# Healthcare Claims Intelligence

## Insights & Recommendations

### Business Question

What should we change next quarter to reduce allowed cost in our top procedure category?

## Executive Summary

Outpatient Procedure is the largest allowed cost category, accounting for 27% of total allowed costs. Spending is driven primarily by high procedure costs rather than high utilization, with four procedures accounting for 85.97% of outpatient allowed cost. Out-of-network services for these procedures cost approximately 20% more than comparable in-network services. Existing out-of-network utilization among the four highest-cost procedures represents a total modeled cost reduction opportunity of approximately $787K. For next quarter, a 25% shift in out-of-network utilization toward in-network care is recommended, representing approximately $197K in modeled savings.

## Key Findings

1. Outpatient Procedure is the largest cost category at $23.68M, or 26.97% of total allowed cost.

2. Outpatient procedures represent only 4.53% of claim lines. High cost per procedure, rather than unusually high utilization, is the primary cost driver.

3. Four procedures account for 85.97% of outpatient allowed cost: laparoscopic cholecystectomy, knee arthroscopy with meniscectomy, anterior abdominal hernia repair, and cataract removal with intraocular lens insertion.

4. Individual provider comparisons are not reliable because procedure volume per provider is sparse, with a median of one line per provider among the highest-cost procedures.

5. Out-of-network allowed amounts are consistently approximately 20% higher than in-network amounts for the four highest-cost procedures.

6. After controlling for procedure and network status, state-level cost variation is generally about 5% to 10%, making geography a secondary cost driver.

7. High-cost members disproportionately receive more expensive procedures. Procedure mix and cost intensity explain much of the member-level cost concentration.

## Cost Opportunity

The four highest-cost procedures have approximately $4.72M in out-of-network spending. Applying observed in-network average allowed amounts to the existing out-of-network utilization produces an estimated total cost opportunity of approximately $787K.

### Out-of-Network Excess Cost by Procedure

- Laparoscopic cholecystectomy: $269.98K
- Knee arthroscopy with meniscectomy: $200.65K
- Anterior abdominal hernia repair: $177.03K
- Cataract removal with intraocular lens insertion: $139.55K
- **Total modeled opportunity: approximately $787.21K**

## Scenario Modeling

- **25% OON shift:** $196.80K estimated savings
- **50% OON shift:** $393.60K estimated savings
- **75% OON shift:** $590.40K estimated savings
- **100% OON shift:** $787.21K estimated savings

## Recommendation

Reduce out-of-network utilization for the four highest-cost outpatient procedures by 25% next quarter by steering appropriate services toward in-network providers. Based on observed cost differences, this represents approximately $197K in modeled savings.

The organization should investigate referral patterns, network availability, member and provider education, and network access for these procedures to determine which out-of-network services can reasonably be shifted in-network.

## Measurement Plan

Track the following KPIs next quarter:

- Out-of-network utilization rate
- Out-of-network claim lines
- Average allowed cost per line
- Total allowed cost
- Estimated savings

## Limitations

The savings estimates are modeled scenarios based on observed allowed-cost differences and should not be interpreted as guaranteed savings. The analysis does not establish why members used out-of-network providers or whether every out-of-network service can be redirected. Individual provider-level conclusions are also limited by sparse procedure volume.

## Conclusion

Next quarter, the strongest cost-reduction opportunity is to increase in-network utilization for the four procedures driving most outpatient procedure spending. Network status shows a larger and more consistent cost difference than geography, provider identity, or utilization frequency, making it the clearest actionable intervention identified in the analysis.
